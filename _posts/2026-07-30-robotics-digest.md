## 7. Policy / standards / safety

**⚠️ HEADLINE — FCC bans imports of new foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots (July 29, 2026)**

The US Federal Communications Commission issued an order immediately banning imports of new models of foreign-made humanoid robots, quadruped ("robot dog") robots, and power inverters, citing cybersecurity and national-security risks to critical infrastructure.

Key facts:
- **Scope:** Applies to robot and inverter models *not yet* authorized for US sale. Previously authorized units may continue to operate.
- **Rationale:** White House task force found foreign-built robots pose "supply chain vulnerabilities" and "cybersecurity risks" to US critical infrastructure.
- **Target:** Primarily China, which holds ~85% global humanoid market share. Unitree specifically named as an affected company — the Pentagon added Unitree to its Chinese military companies list six weeks ago.
- **Diplomatic stakes:** The ban is expected to stress China-US relations ahead of a planned Xi-Trump summit in September.
- **Legislative backdrop:** The American Security Robotics Act (Cotton/Schumer/Stefanik) and the GUARD Act are companion Congressional vehicles moving in parallel.

Sources: [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/29/tech/us-china-robot-ban-intl-hnk) | [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2026/07/28/united-states-bans-chinese-humanoid--quadruped-robots-citing-national-security/) | [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/29/us-bans-imports-of-new-chinese-robots-over-security-concerns) | [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/5996462-fcc-bans-foreign-humanoid-robots/) | [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/humanoid-robots-imports-us-ban-china-national-security-concerns/)

## So what — strategic implications

- **FCC ban = forced market restructuring.** With China locked out of new US humanoid/quadruped imports, every US or EU customer that wanted a Unitree product must now source from non-Chinese vendors. Figure, Agility, 1X, Boston Dynamics, and NEURA are the immediate beneficiaries. Their compute suppliers — NVIDIA (Jetson Thor), Qualcomm (Dragonwing IQ10), and Intel (Core Ultra + OpenVINO) — all get incremental addressable demand. **Intel's angle:** the ban is a structural argument for US-silicon-on-US-robot stacks.

- **Unitree AS2-W timing is not a coincidence.** Dropping a new platform the same day the US bans its products is a message to non-US markets (Europe, ASEAN, Middle East) that China's innovation pace continues unabated. Watch the European distribution push (H1 Pro launched commercially July 22) as Unitree tries to lock in Western customers before further restrictions spread.

- **Tau Robotics' teleoperation model is the near-term commercialization template.** At $30/hr with human-in-the-loop, Tau sidesteps the autonomy reliability bar that's blocking competitors from going commercial. The data collection dividend (home-environment training footage at scale) is the real asset. Expect more US startups to follow this pattern in Q3/Q4.

- **Taiwan chipmakers accelerating into robot silicon** (per Digitimes July 28-29) signals that the competitive silicon landscape below Jetson Thor is about to get much noisier. MediaTek, Realtek, and fabless startups are eyeing the mid-tier robot segment — where Intel's Core Ultra NPU actually competes. **Watch for Computex-to-production timelines** on any Taiwan robot-AI silicon that was demoed in June.
