## So what — strategic implications

- **Chinese OEMs self-designing humanoids** is the week's structural signal: Dongfeng's announcement means the customer/supplier dynamic in China is collapsing. Chinese humanoid startups (Unitree, Fourier, Agibot) now face OEM in-sourcing risk *on top of* the US import ban imposed in July. This compresses the viable export market and raises urgency for differentiated IP (silicon, software stack, BOM cost).
- **Tomorrow's NVIDIA ROS 2 keynote at ROSCon is the Intel-pressure watch item**: hardware-accelerated zero-copy transport natively on Jetson would deepen middleware lock-in, making it harder for OpenVINO/Intel-based compute to compete on developer toolchain integration. Watch for whether NVIDIA explicitly targets Jetson Thor modules as the reference platform.
- **IROS 2026 next week** (Pittsburgh) is the major inflection point: 1,900 papers and 170+ exhibitors in the world's second-largest robotics research venue. Expect silicon, software, and deployment announcements to cluster there — plan a deeper sweep.
- **Thin domestic US news today** contrasts with China's continued pace: Dongfeng is the fourth OEM deploying in-house humanoids this year, while no US automaker has announced equivalent in-house robotics manufacturing. The gap in deployment velocity is widening.
