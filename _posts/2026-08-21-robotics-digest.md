## So what — strategic implications

- **Unitree's $50B public valuation is the sector's new price anchor.** Every private humanoid company, and every edge-compute supplier pitching into robotics, now has a comparable. Intel's hybrid-edge story needs to quantify cost advantage per-unit at Unitree/AgiBot production volumes — not just per-system.
- **FCC ban creates a structural US-market opening for domestic compute.** US-assembled robots qualifying for the 65% domestic-content threshold will need domestically-sourced silicon. Intel Core Ultra (Intel fabs) has a sourcing argument that Jetson Thor (TSMC) may not — but Intel needs to move quickly before Qualcomm or Ambarella claim this positioning.
- **Hot Chips (Aug 23–25) is the month's most important Intel-pressure test.** NVIDIA's Vera Rubin architectural deep-dive will give platform decision-makers the TOPS/W numbers they need. If Blackwell's edge envelope decisively beats Core Ultra NPU on efficiency at comparable price points, the narrative hardens and customer design wins follow.
- **Watch WRC Developer Day (today) and Robot Games (Aug 22–26) for software partnerships.** China's government co-timing these events is deliberate. Expect AgiBot, Unitree, and industrial OEMs to announce compute and software stack partnerships by the end of the week — these will signal which silicon and middleware (Isaac vs. ROS 2 vs. OpenVINO) are winning in Chinese humanoid production deployments.
