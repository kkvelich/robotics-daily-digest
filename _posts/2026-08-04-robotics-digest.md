## So what — strategic implications

1. **FCC ban just got real.** Canceling Unitree's North American H1 Pro launch is the first concrete commercial consequence — moving the ban from policy text to market reality. US robotics OEMs (Figure, Agility, Boston Dynamics, Apptronik) gain a protected window in the NA market, but they need to fill it: Unitree's Asia/Europe trajectory shows what un-protected competition looks like at scale.

2. **Unitree's week (Aug 5–10) is the most data-dense moment in China humanoid robotics this year**: Asia launch + IPO price discovery + Humanoids conference + IPO subscription. Success on all fronts cements Unitree as the market's proof-of-concept for profitable humanoid manufacturing. Failure on any axis (IPO priced below floor, subscription shortfall) will reset China sector sentiment.

3. **SERV Q2 (Aug 6) is the week's real economic data point for robotics deployment.** If Serve can show improving revenue-per-robot metrics despite its YTD stock collapse, it validates the RaaS model for the broader sector. A miss on unit economics will sharpen investors' questions for every humanoid company claiming commercial deployment.

4. **Intel's edge-robotics window narrows before Hot Chips.** Two weeks until HC38 and Intel has no public response to: AMD Ryzen AI X100 claims (2.1× Core Ultra S3 multithread, 3× Jetson Thor FP32), the NXP+Ambarella combo (if it closes), or Microchip's Hailo acquisition. The HC38 NPU session is now a high-stakes moment — silence or vague roadmap language will be read as a gap by system integrators making H2 BOM decisions.
