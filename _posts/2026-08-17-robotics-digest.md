## So what — strategic implications

- **Unitree's first-day trading print matters beyond China.** If derivatives-implied 4x holds, the market is pricing the global robotics hardware opportunity at a level that will pressure US humanoid valuations (Figure at $39B pre-revenue vs. Unitree at $9B profitable). A strong debut cements Chinese hardware as the global cost floor that all US players must respond to in BOM and SWaP-C terms.

- **CASE 2026 in Shenyang is a soft-power signal as much as a technical one.** China hosting a top-3 IEEE-RAS automation conference in its industrial core is not incidental. Expect papers from MIIT-funded labs, Estun/Siasun spinouts, and embodied-AI programs that rarely appear in Western venues — the technical program is worth scanning for early deployment data from factory humanoid trials.

- **The edge-silicon narrative is quiet ahead of the storm.** No new chip announcements today. But Hot Chips is six days away: if Intel surfaces any Core Ultra Series 3 / NPU robotics roadmap detail beyond what shipped at Computex, that is the first public update in 90 days. Equally, if NVIDIA uses the venue to position Cosmos 3 Edge (4B-parameter on-device world model) against Intel's VLA inference claims, that sets the competitive framing for H2 2026. Watch closely.

- **Light day = good time to track the FCC fallout.** The foreign-robot import ban (eff. July 28) is now three weeks old. Import disruption signals — delayed shipments, Dept. of War conditional-approval filings, or domestic AMR/humanoid RFQ spikes at US OEMs — should start appearing in trade press this week. Any named US buyer switching from a China-sourced AMR to a domestic alternative is a deployment signal worth flagging.
